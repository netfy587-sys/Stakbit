Houseplant care in five simple habits

Most plant trouble comes from light and water. Get these five right and the rest is easy.

☀️

Give it the right light

Most houseplants like bright, indirect light. Direct midday sun can scorch leaves, while a dark corner makes plants stretch and weaken.

💧

Water only when needed

Overwatering is a very common cause of plant loss. Push a finger into the soil, and water when the top few centimetres feel dry.

🪴

Choose pots with drainage

Holes at the bottom let extra water escape so roots do not sit in wet soil. Pick a pot only slightly bigger than the root ball.

🌡️

Keep conditions steady

Avoid cold draughts, heaters and sudden changes. Most common houseplants prefer normal room temperatures.

🌿

Feed gently in the growing season

A diluted plant food in spring and summer is enough for most plants. Ease off in autumn and winter when growth slows.

👀

Check in weekly

Look under leaves for pests, turn the pot for even light and remove dry leaves. Small, regular attention beats big rescues.

What your plant is telling you 💬

Leaves give clues. Here are common signs and the usual causes.

Yellow, soft leavesOften too much water or poor drainage. Let the soil dry and check the pot holes.
Crispy brown tipsUsually dry air, irregular watering or too much plant food.
Long, leggy growthNot enough light. Move it closer to a bright window.
Drooping leavesCheck the soil first. Both very dry and waterlogged soil can cause it.

A simple seasonal rhythm 🗓️

🌸 Spring

Growth restarts. Repot if roots are crowded and begin light feeding.

☀️ Summer

Water more often and shield plants from harsh midday sun.

🍂 Autumn

Reduce feeding and watch for pests before moving plants indoors.

❄️ Winter

Water less, keep plants away from cold glass and heaters.

Snake plants, pothos, ZZ plants and spider plants are forgiving choices for most homes.

How often should I water?
